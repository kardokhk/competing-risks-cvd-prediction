## How to read the intervals

Every interval is a 95% interval from a paired shared-index bootstrap (B = 1000) pooled across m = 30 imputations. Two intervals are reported for every quantity:

- **percentile** (columns `lo`, `hi`): the 2.5th and 97.5th percentiles of the pooled 30 x 1000 bootstrap distribution. This is Schomaker and Heumann's MI Boot (PS) and it is the interval quoted below, because several of these quantities are not close to normal: E/O is a ratio and a net benefit near zero is bounded below by the treat-none line.
- **Rubin** (columns `lo_rubin`, `hi_rubin`): Qbar plus or minus a t quantile on Barnard-Rubin degrees of freedom times the square root of the total variance. Reported alongside; where the two disagree materially the statistic is not normal and the percentile interval is the one to use.

The point estimate is always Qbar, the mean over imputations of the estimate on the unbootstrapped imputed data. It is never the mean of the bootstrap distribution. `src/v2/08_pool_and_report.py` has the formulae.

## 2. The full Van Calster calibration hierarchy

**A caveat on version 1's slope values.** Version 1's `calib_slope` came from an ordinary least squares fit of ten decile means on the complementary log-log scale (`src/eval/eval_lib.R`, `calib_slope_intercept`). That discards all but ten points, has no valid standard error, and is not the calibration slope any guideline defines. Its values, including the Fine-Gray slope of 2.02 and the naive random forest slope of 0.93 that the audit quoted, are therefore not a sound basis for saying which family calibrates better on slope. Version 2 uses the pseudo-observation model of van Geloven et al. and reports it below; where the two estimators disagree, the disagreement is about the estimator, not about the models.

Version 1 computed the calibration slope and intercept and reported neither. It reported E/O and ICI, the two levels on which the competing-risk models look better. All three levels are below. If the ordering reverses between levels, that reversal is the finding.

## 2b. Does the ICI difference survive a concordant smoother?

Austin and Putter (Stat Med 2026;45:e70468, doi:10.1002/sim.70468) recommend that the smoothed event probabilities behind ICI, E50 and E90 come from a model concordant with the type of model whose calibration is being assessed. Their two types are the Fine-Gray subdistribution model and the pair of cause-specific hazard models, and in their Weibull cause-specific-hazard scenarios a discordant smoother raised the mean ICI from 0.018 to 0.024, always away from zero. A discordant smoother manufactures apparent miscalibration, so it can manufacture an ICI difference between two families as well.

Version 1 used one local Aalen-Johansen smoother for every model. Since the manuscript leads with a naive-versus-competing ICI difference, that choice had to be tested rather than assumed. Four smoothers are reported for every model:

| smoother | model | concordant with |
|---|---|---|
| pseudo-observation, complementary log-log mean link (`ici`) | nonparametric jackknife pseudo-values of the Aalen-Johansen CIF | neither type. This is what van Geloven et al. BMJ 2022 use and what makes version 2 comparable with version 1. |
| `ici_smoothfg` | `FGR(Hist(time, status) ~ rcs(cloglog(p), 3 knots), cause = 1)` | a Fine-Gray model |
| `ici_smoothcsh` | `CSC(Hist(time, status) ~ rcs(cloglog(p), 3 knots), cause = 1)` | cause-specific hazard models |
| `ici_smoothnet` | `coxph(Surv(time, status == 1) ~ rcs(cloglog(p), 3 knots))`, risk = 1 - S(t), competing death censored | how a **naive** prediction was made. **This is an extension, not in the paper.** Austin and Putter consider only models predicting a cumulative incidence; a naive model predicts 1 - S(t), so the smoother estimating that same quantity is the concordant one. |

Knots follow Austin, Putter, Lee and van Buuren (Diagn Progn Res 2022;6:2, doi:10.1186/s41512-021-00114-6): three knots at the 10th, 50th and 90th percentiles of cloglog(predicted risk). `ici_concordant_austin` is whichever of the three is concordant with that model's type, and is missing for `rsf_cr`, `logistic_cr`, `deephit` and `nfg`, which are neither Fine-Gray nor cause-specific hazard models and for which the argument does not determine a smoother.

`ici_concordant` is a separate and coarser check: the same pseudo-observation smoother, but with the observed quantity matched to the model's estimand, the Aalen-Johansen CIF for a competing-risk model and 1 - S(t) for a naive one.

## 4. Decision thresholds, and how many people they concern

The endpoint here is cardiovascular **mortality**, whose observed 10-year cumulative incidence in this cohort is about 3.7%. Applying an incident-disease threshold to a mortality endpoint interrogates a region of the risk distribution that is nearly empty. ## 5. The named defects, and what fixing each one changed

### Cycle-stratified censoring weights

Censoring in this cohort is administrative: it is the calendar gap between the examination and the end of mortality linkage on 31 December 2019. It ranges from 0% to essentially the whole cycle before 10 y. A marginal Kaplan-Meier censoring distribution averages hazards that do not overlap. Every inverse-probability-of-censoring weight here is estimated within cycle; the marginal version is computed alongside so the size of the change is visible.

| split | metric | median cycle-stratified | median marginal | median difference | largest absolute difference |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| cv | auc | 0.8228 | 0.8300 | -0.0074 | 0.0080 |
| cv | cindex | 0.8315 | 0.8254 | +0.0052 | 0.0064 |
| temporal | auc | 0.8247 | 0.8254 | -0.0008 | 0.0013 |
| temporal | cindex | 0.8242 | 0.8238 | +0.0003 | 0.0006 |

The change is material (largest absolute shift 0.0080). It is reported either way: a defect that turns out not to matter is still a defect that was fixed, and the reader is entitled to know which.

### Cross-validation pseudoreplication

Version 1's `src/eval/run_eval_cv.R` pooled 5 repeats of some models and 2 of others into one table and treated the rows as independent, which inflated the effective sample size by roughly the number of repeats and made models with different repeat counts directly comparable when they are not. Version 2 uses three repeats for every model, computes the statistic within each repeat on a complete out-of-fold prediction for all 41,151 subjects, and averages the three. Repeat is a nested factor. The bootstrap resamples subjects, and a resampled subject carries all of its repeats.

### At-risk fraction, everywhere

| split | horizon | fraction of the sample informative at this horizon |
|---|---|---|
| cv | 5 y | 79.8% |
| cv | 10 y | 55.3% |
| cv | 15 y | 33.8% |
| temporal | 5 y | 100.0% |
| temporal | 10 y | 77.9% |

A subject is informative at horizon t if followed to t or if an event of either cause occurred before t. Version 1's headline 15-year stratum had 7.9% of its stratum at risk. Any stratified result reported from these files must carry this column, and any stratum with fewer than 15 events is flagged in `calibration/deciles__*.csv` and should be dropped rather than interpreted.

## 8. What is not settled

1. **Sufficiency of m.** The largest FMI over the estimands quoted above is 0.156, which by von Hippel's rule would require m = 12; m = 30 was used. That is sufficient.

2. **smcfcs was not run.** Substantive-model-compatible FCS is the recommended imputation approach for cause-specific competing risks; it could not be installed in this environment. Bonneville et al. 2025 find it comparable to the MICE predictor set used here for cumulative-incidence prediction, which is the target, but that is a citation and not a check on this data.

3. **The survey design is applied at the analysis stage, not in the imputation.** Kim et al. 2006 show that combination is not self-efficient. The size of the resulting bias is not quantified here.

4. **The C-index discretises the risk axis** into 1,024 quantile bins so that a 2,000-replicate bootstrap is affordable. `validation/comparison__*.csv` gives the agreement with `pec::cindex` on the point estimate.

5. **Decision curves carry no confidence band.** The intervals that carry the argument are at the pre-specified thresholds in `main_metrics.csv` and `paired_contrasts.csv`. A band on every point of a decision curve reads as a family of simultaneous intervals, which it is not.

6. **15-year results exist only for the cross-validated split** and only where the at-risk fraction supports them. The temporal test set is cycles 2007-2010 against mortality linkage ending 31 December 2019, so its maximum follow-up is 13.2 y.
